Lesson 01 · Start here

One input can travel a long way.

The app connects everyday work—bills, payments, invoices and entries—to balances and reports. Learn when a record starts counting, who handles it next, and where to look for its effect.

Start with the purchase story in lessons 4–6. It explains most of what we built with Claude. You can come back for sales, journals and BIR reports afterward.

The purchase lifecycle

  1. SupplierSave their identity and usual terms.No amount is owed yet.
  2. Draft billEnter their invoice and attach it.Still not in the books.
  3. Approval & tax checkSomeone else reviews it.Tax check only with withholding.
  4. Posted billIt becomes money owed.Books and purchase reports can read it.
  5. Payment voucherPrepare, then release payment.Preparation reserves; release pays.

Records versus reports

A bill is a record you enter. Aging and the purchase list are views calculated from eligible records. Fix the source when a report is wrong; do not type the same transaction again to make a total look right.

Capability versus readiness

A working invoice screen does not settle where the company must issue official invoices. Oojeema remains the registered accounting/invoicing system in our recorded arrangements. Production use and report acceptance still need the agreed setup and review.

Check yourself: does saving a supplier mean we owe them money?

No. A supplier is a contact/setup record. A bill must clear the approval path before it counts as owed. Existing unpaid balances also need the planned opening-data process.

There are two checks: your permission, and the record’s current state. A person allowed to approve bills still cannot approve their own submission. A posted bill is not editable. Ask what state it is in before assuming access is broken.

No. The bill moves only through its allowed record action. Reading is just notification housekeeping.

What are debit and credit, in the examples here?

They are the two sides of an accounting entry, not labels for “good/bad” or always “in/out.” A bill can debit an expense and input VAT while crediting amounts owed. A payment can debit the payable account and credit money in the bank.

The approved journal must balance: total debit equals total credit. That catches an unequal entry, but not a wrong supplier, account, date or tax category.

CASH, money accounts and choosing where payment comes from

The imported chart can group banks and wallets beneath a CASH parent. Voucher choices use the existing paying-account rules: active children under CASH plus configured defaults; if that grouping is absent, the existing fallback offers asset accounts. Check the selected account carefully.

The Cash Disbursements Journal includes all descendants beneath CASH plus the configured money defaults, including inactive historical accounts. Bank reconciliation offers active eligible accounts and also retains bank/cash-named asset eligibility. These are related but not identical selection rules.

A parent grouping is not evidence that every child should be used for a particular payment. Account setup still needs Finance review.

Important starting-data distinction: importing the chart creates account definitions. It does not bring in their opening balances. Importing suppliers creates supplier records, not existing bills or debts.
Check yourself: the chart has the correct bank account. Does that mean its opening money is recorded?

No. Definition and opening balance are separate. The cut-off date and opening data remain an important readiness decision.

Our fictional example

Sample Learning Supplies sends an invoice for ₱11,200 including VAT. For this example only, its line has ₱10,000 before VAT, ₱1,200 VAT and ₱100 withholding. The supplier is due ₱11,100.

The illustrative 1% withholding is not a rule for every supplier. The applicable code/rate and treatment must come from checked setup and Sir Mel’s review.

Supplier import: what arrives, and what does not?

Choose the vendor CSV, read the summary and confirm Import directory once. You do not need to review every vendor. Clear names, contacts, billing addresses, terms and usable TINs arrive automatically. Existing suppliers and repeated records are skipped; identity conflicts and unusable names are excluded with a summary.

Questionable TINs and other source hints stay on the supplier under Imported details kept for later. Tax defaults remain unconfirmed; this directory is not a certificate of BIR readiness. Unknown defaults do not fill bill tax choices. When preparing an actual bill, select its VAT and withholding and confirm the document before submission. Users with Edit suppliers may optionally save confirmed defaults for future bills. Original posted bills are unchanged.

Column coverage and exceptions are optional details. Source IDs, bills, payments and opening balances are not imported. If an import outcome is uncertain, recheck the summary before retrying.

What is a default, and what is a saved transaction?

Picking a supplier can fill terms and the usual line VAT/withholding choices. Once lines are entered and saved, read the actual bill values. Do not assume that changing a supplier’s defaults later rewrites every earlier bill.

The form’s running calculation is a guide. The server validates the bill and calculates the saved totals. A blank or invalid field can stop saving; a missing dated VAT rate can prevent calculation.

This area interacts with

Accounts and tax setup for calculations and posting choices → bill approval for review → Payables for amounts owed → purchase/withholding reports only when the bill is posted.

Check yourself: does selecting “Importation” mean customs VAT and supplier advances are fully handled?

No. It classifies the line for the current breakdown. The wider importation process remains to be confirmed and built.

Lesson 05 · The hand-off

A bill starts counting only when it clears.

The agreed flow is: Aubrey prepares → Malou approves, with Cristal as backup → Sir Rommel checks if withholding exists → the bill is posted and ready to pay.

“Approved” and “posted” can be different moments. With withholding, Malou’s approval is not the end: Sir Rommel’s check still stands between that bill and the books.
What gets recorded when our example clears?

The illustrative entry is: expense/asset ₱10,000 and input VAT ₱1,200 on the debit side; supplier payable ₱11,100 and withholding payable ₱100 on the credit side. Both sides total ₱11,200.

The bill’s approved/checked details, history and applicable electronic signatures remain available. Purchase reports read the eligible posted bill, while account reports read the journal. You do not enter an additional manual journal for the same purchase.

Backup approval, editing and concurrent actions

“Cristal is backup” means they have the relevant approval permission; it does not permit self-approval. The approver still cannot check the same bill’s withholding.

Draft/returned bills can be corrected under existing edit permissions. Once posted, the bill is voided and entered again when appropriate, subject to payment restrictions. If somebody acted first, the server can refuse an old action and ask you to reload. A button shown earlier is not a guarantee it can still be used.

Who sees both sides?

Aubrey sees “My bills waiting for approval” or “waiting for the tax check.” Malou/Cristal see eligible approval work; Sir Rommel sees eligible checks. After posting, Cristal can find the bill available for payment preparation. The queues follow permissions and identity, not only the person’s job title.

Check yourself: Malou approves a bill with withholding. Should its amount appear in accounts payable aging immediately?

Not until the tax check clears it. While waiting, it remains outside the posted amount owed. After posting, it enters current AP aging if unpaid.

Lesson 06 · From liability to payment

Reserved money is still owed money.

A payment voucher says which cleared bills are being paid, how much of each, how payment is made, and which money account supplies it. One voucher can pay several bills for the same supplier.

  1. Choose cleared billsOnly eligible amounts.Existing reservations reduce the amount offered.
  2. Draft voucherEnter allocations and payment method.Amounts are reserved.
  3. SubmitReady for release.Still unpaid.
  4. ReleaseRecord actual payment details.Posts payment and reduces the debt.
  5. Proof & trackingReview history and documents.Partly paid or paid bill.
What you enterWhat it controls
Supplier and billsSelect the cleared bills being settled. A pending approval does not become payable by putting it on a voucher.
Amount per billFull or partial allocation. The total voucher amount is the sum of these allocations; do not count each allocation as a separate voucher.
Method and paid-from accountBank transfer, check or cash; the exact eligible account. This is where the payment journal credits money.
References / notesOojeema/Zoho references for tracing; bank reference or check details at release. These do not synchronize to another system.
Payment date and evidenceWhen the released payment is recorded and its supporting proof. A prepared voucher is not bank evidence that money moved.
Release records the payment in Finance. It does not send a bank transfer, clear a check at the bank, or confirm the supplier received funds. The actual bank/cash process and evidence still matter.

A posted bill is owed; no voucher has reserved or paid it yet.

Why a second voucher cannot simply pay the same amount again

Open vouchers reserve their bill allocations, including drafts, returned vouchers and legacy waiting stages. A second voucher can use only what is not already paid or reserved. A returned voucher still holds its allocation until it is changed or cancelled.

The server checks payment eligibility again at release. Preparing or downloading a voucher is not proof of payment. The person who prepared a voucher may release it if they hold release permission; the bill approvals have already happened.

Cancel versus void—and why withholding is not repeated

Cancel is for an unreleased voucher: no payment was posted, so cancellation frees its reservation. Void is for a released voucher: it requires the relevant permission, records the reversal and makes those bill amounts owed again.

Release posts a debit to the bill’s payable account and a credit to the money account. The purchase’s VAT and withholding were already established on the bill. Payment does not recalculate them as a second purchase.

Accounts payable aging: how late is the remaining debt?

Open Payables → Accounts payable aging. It includes unpaid/part-paid posted bills, grouped by supplier. The five buckets are not yet due (including today), 1–30, 31–60, 61–90 and over 90 days late. Age comes from the due date; the amount is what remains payable after recorded payments.

Click a supplier, bucket or oldest bill to inspect the source. The CSV follows the selected supplier/bucket. Inactive suppliers remain if money is still owed.

This A+B aging page shows current balances aged today. It does not reconstruct what was owed on an older date. A payment made later cannot simply be subtracted from a historical balance without reconstructing history.

This area interacts with

Cleared bills → voucher reservations → released payment journal → bill paid/remaining amounts → AP aging and Overview. The money journal also feeds the Cash Disbursements Journal and the chosen bank account’s movements for reconciliation.

Check yourself: one ₱5,000 voucher is waiting for release. How much does the supplier still count as owed on our ₱11,100 bill?

₱11,100. ₱5,000 is reserved, and ₱6,100 is available for another preparation. Only release reduces the recorded debt.

Lesson 07 · The customer side

A sale and its collection are separate events.

An issued invoice records the sale and what the customer owes. A payment records how that amount was settled. Receivables means money customers still owe us; payables means money we owe suppliers.

Software capability is not permission to replace official invoicing. Our recorded arrangement keeps official invoices in Oojeema. How sales enter Finance at production scale—import or issue—still needs a decision.
  1. Draft invoiceCustomer, lines and tax details.No issued sale yet.
  2. IssueValidate setup and assign invoice number.Posts sale and customer debt.
  3. Record collectionCash received plus any customer withholding.Reduces what the customer owes.
  4. Receivables & reportsReview unpaid amounts and sales trail.Sales and receipts use different source events.

Example: a customer pays partly in cash and partly through withholding

An illustrative invoice totals ₱2,240. The customer sends ₱2,200 and withholds ₱40, with the required tax code/details. The settled amount is ₱2,240, while money received is ₱2,200. The ₱40 is not a bank deposit and is not supplier withholding we owe.

Correcting a sale: cancel-and-reissue is not a credit memo

Issued invoices are not edited in place. The cancel-and-reissue path records the reason, cancels the original as permitted and creates a replacement draft linked to it. Check and issue that draft as required; a new draft is not automatically an issued replacement.

Returns, credit memos and refunds are a separate missing workflow on the readiness list. Do not use cancellation or a manual entry as an undocumented substitute for it.

Receivables aging and its current limit

Receivables groups open issued invoices by how late they are, using invoice date plus terms for the due date. Payments reduce the current unpaid amount.

The existing receivables endpoint accepts a date but currently uses today’s recorded paid amount. Treat past-date results cautiously; accurate historical reconstruction has not been established. This is why the new supplier aging is explicitly current-day only.

This area interacts with

CRM customer lookup and tax setup → invoice → sales/receivable journal → collections and customer withholding → receivables, Sales Journal, Cash Receipts, sales list, VAT figures and customer-withholding list. The Sales DAT converter is a different file-processing route.

Check yourself: the customer withholds ₱40. Should that ₱40 appear as money deposited into our bank?

No. It settles part of the invoice but is tracked separately from cash received. The supporting withholding details matter.

Do not record the same transaction twice.

Our purchase example as two separate entries

When bill clears: debit expense/asset ₱10,000; debit input VAT ₱1,200; credit supplier payable ₱11,100; credit withholding payable ₱100.

When ₱5,000 payment releases: debit supplier payable ₱5,000; credit selected money account ₱5,000. The bill now has ₱6,100 left. The purchase expense and VAT are not booked again.

Different source events explain why the Purchase Journal and Cash Disbursements Journal are not the same report.

Manual journals and purchase tax detail: a journal can affect account totals, but the SLP purchase list and quarterly input VAT calculation currently read bills. A journal to an input-VAT account alone does not supply a supplier’s purchase lines, identity or category to those bill-based reports.

The bank reconciliation lifecycle

  1. StatementSelect account, statement date and ending balance.Use the right account.
  2. Book movementsInspect unmatched recorded movements.Mark those shown by the bank.
  3. Resolve differenceRecord missing charges/interest properly first.Do not invent a matching tick.
  4. FinishOnly when the difference is zero.Cleared movements stay linked.
What a zero reconciliation difference does—and does not—prove

The screen compares its opening statement balance plus selected incoming money minus selected outgoing money with the statement ending balance. An unfinished reconciliation can be discarded under its existing rules. A finished one provides the starting point for the next statement.

The reconciliation does not pull a live bank feed or create a missing charge just because you typed the bank’s ending balance. A missing transaction must go through its proper recording/approval process and then appear in the books. Matching the balance still requires checking that the selected movements are the right ones.

Check yourself: a posted bill already created an expense entry. Should you add a manual entry for the same invoice to make it show in reports?

No. That would duplicate the accounting effect. First check the bill’s status, dates, filters and report source.

An advance is not yet an accepted expense.

Example: ₱3,000 advanced, ₱2,500 accepted

The unused ₱500 is due back. Finishing review identifies that amount; it does not mean the ₱500 has already been handed back. Record the refund when it is actually received. If accepted company-covered spending exceeds the advance, the result can instead be reimbursement due.

Why release or review may be blocked

The person who released an advance cannot review its liquidation, including an Administrator. The requester’s earlier advance still awaiting liquidation can hold a new release. A liquidation already submitted and waiting on Finance review does not hold the next release under the current rule; one sent back to the requester for correction does.

Finishing review requires an accept/reject decision and category on every expense. Read the specific error rather than changing roles to bypass it. When Malou or another reviewer is needed, use the agreed hand-off.

Sections connected

CRM → Marketing requests → release/review/settlement queues → expense categories and default accounts → journals, spending views and financial books. Supplier-specific purchase tax details are not automatically created in the bill-based SLP/quarterly VAT route.

Product price approvals are a separate lifecycle

  1. Price request in CRMSomebody proposes a price.Origin stays in CRM.
  2. Finance price queueRead the requested price and context.Requires price-view access.
  3. Allowed decisionApprove or return/reject as offered.Requires approval permission.
  4. CRM resultThe connected request receives the outcome.Not a supplier bill or invoice payment.
Empty, unavailable and waiting are different

An empty connected queue can mean nothing is waiting. An unavailable connection means the app could not check. The Overview asks for CRM price counts separately so it does not have to wait on that service for all Finance work. A connection error must not be interpreted as a verified count of zero.

Price approval is not the same as maintaining company tax rates, and it does not itself issue a sales invoice.

Check yourself: Cristal released the advance. Can they formally review that same liquidation just because they hold broad access?

No. The release/review separation rule applies independently of broad access.

Names you will see, in everyday language

Input VAT: purchase-side VAT recorded in the supported bill flow. Output VAT: sales-side VAT. EWT: expanded withholding tax, such as amounts withheld from suppliers. ATC: the tax classification code used with withholding. These explanations describe the app’s inputs; Sir Mel must verify the applicable treatment.

QAP: Quarterly Alphalist of Payees, grouping supplier withholding details. 2307: a withholding certificate; note whether we issue one to a supplier or receive one from a customer. DAT: a structured file accepted by a particular submission/validation process, not simply any spreadsheet with a renamed extension.

The 1601-EQ view does not prove what was remitted

The current quarterly view uses the first two months’ calculated 0619-E withholding figures when showing an indicative remainder. Its own note says those are what the books show withheld, and actual remittances may differ. Confirm actual remitted amounts with Sir Mel; do not treat the calculated subtraction as a payment ledger.

Why can my transaction be missing?
  1. Is it only a draft or still waiting for approval/check?
  2. Are you looking at the right company and date/quarter/fiscal period?
  3. Does the report read that kind of record? A journal and a supplier bill are different sources.
  4. Was it voided, replaced, or entered only in another system?
  5. Are supplier/customer identity, VAT and withholding details complete?
  6. Did you prepare a payment rather than release it?

Check the record and its history before adding another transaction. Keep unresolved differences for review instead of forcing totals.

Current outputs need Sir Mel’s test-data review and a comparison with a real filed period before relying on them for filing. Several submission formats and recording routes remain unfinished. CSV download is not BIR submission, and report generation is not tax payment.
Check yourself: you converted an Oojeema Sales DAT file. Why might Finance’s Sales Journal remain unchanged?

The converter does not import transactions. Its output is a file, not a set of Finance invoices. A separate sales-ingestion decision and workflow is still needed.

Use Print / Save as PDF on report or record views, or the existing document Print button for invoices and vouchers. How the people fit, without treating names as hard-coded permissions

Our recorded local arrangement: Aubrey prepares bills; Malou approves with Cristal as backup; Sir Rommel checks withholding; Cristal prepares/releases payments. IT keeps Administrator access. Those are the agreed responsibilities, while the code checks actual permissions and record identity.

Later learning sessions will narrow the guide by role/permission. This edition first explains the whole system. A learning filter would never grant live access.

Signing in to confirm identity

Some signature actions require identity confirmation. People using Bishnoi One sign-in may be confirmed by a recent sign-in under the implemented rules rather than a Finance password they do not know. Do not share credentials or set somebody else’s signature to work around this.

Check yourself: does a reminder marked done mean the bill was paid?

No. Calendar completion tracks the event. Only the bill/payment workflow determines whether the supplier was recorded as paid.

Small glossary
Accounts payable (AP)
Money we owe suppliers on posted bills.
Accounts receivable (AR)
Money customers owe us on issued invoices.
Posted
Recorded into the accounting books through the relevant cleared/approved action.
Liquidation
Accounting for an advance by providing spending details and evidence; then checking and settling any difference.
Reversal / void
A recorded correction that undoes an earlier accounting effect while retaining its trail, under the relevant workflow.
Aging
Grouping remaining debt by how long it is past its due date.
Fiscal period
The reporting period under the company’s configured financial year; it need not be the same assumption for every company.
Running locally and keeping this guide useful

From the repository folder, the three service commands are npm.cmd run bishnoi-platform, npm.cmd run bishnoi-finance and npm.cmd run bishnoi-one-app, in separate terminals. Check what is already running before starting duplicates. Open local Finance. Do not run these commands from the HTML itself.

During this build session Finance was restarted without auto-reload for stability; subsequent backend code edits need a deliberate service restart. Recheck process ownership before stopping anything.

Learning progress is optional browser-local storage. It can disappear when the browser clears file data or the file is moved. It does not synchronize, affect permissions or certify competence. Keep a copy of the HTML; updates should carry a new checked date/version.
